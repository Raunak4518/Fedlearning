\section{The results, read table by table}\label{ch:results}

The results follow the order of the paper's results section. Unless a caption says otherwise, a cell is the mean
$\pm$ standard deviation over seeds 3--5, a difference is FedECS-LT minus the other method, paired over the seeds both
have, and ``significant'' means $p<0.05$ after Holm's correction (\cref{sec:s-stats-design}). The noise floor is
\R{repeat/bal/floor} points for heads as trained and \R{repeat/er/floor} with the readout.

Two labels recur. \emph{FedECS-LT} is the full method, ending with the exact readout. \emph{FedECS-LT (heads only)},
the paper's table label, is the same run before the readout: FedECS-LT's heads as trained. Every other method appears
with its heads as trained in the main tables, as its authors defined it; in \cref{sec:s-same-readout} the readout is
added to the methods of the GeFL-F pipeline.

\paragraph{The numbers} On CIFAR-10-LT FedECS-LT reaches \R{main/cifar10/FedECS-LT/bal}, against

\R{main/cifar10/diff/GeFL-F} \R{main/cifar10/ci/GeFL-F} and \R{main/cifar100/diff/GeFL-F}

of the models of \R{main/cifar100/strongest}.

\paragraph{The heads as trained} Before the readout the heads of FedECS-LT reach
\R{main/cifar10/FedECS-LT (heads only)/bal} and \R{main/cifar100/FedECS-LT (heads only)/bal}. That is
\R{main/cifar10/heads_margin} points ahead of \R{main/cifar10/heads_strongest} on CIFAR-10-LT, with
$p=\R{main/cifar10/heads_strongest_p}$, not significant, and \R{main/cifar100/heads_margin} ahead on CIFAR-100-LT, with
$p=\R{main/cifar100/heads_strongest_p}$, significant. The readout itself adds \R{main/cifar10/FedECS-LT/gain_er} and
\R{main/cifar100/FedECS-LT/gain_er} points to the heads. Of the \R{main/cifar10/margin}-point margin on CIFAR-10-LT,
therefore, about a third comes from the heads and two thirds from the readout.

\paragraph{The other benchmarks} On MNIST, Fashion-MNIST and SVHN the pattern is the same: FedECS-LT reaches
\R{main/mnist/FedECS-LT/bal}, \R{main/fmnist/FedECS-LT/bal} and \R{main/svhn/FedECS-LT/bal}, ahead of the strongest
other method by \R{main/mnist/margin}, \R{main/fmnist/margin} and \R{main/svhn/margin} points (Holm-corrected
$p=\R{main/mnist/strongest_p}$, $\R{main/fmnist/strongest_p}$ and $\R{main/svhn/strongest_p}$; Fashion-MNIST's is not
significant). FedECS-LT has the highest mean on \R{main/nbest_of} long-tailed benchmarks.

\paragraph{New client splits} Seeds 6--8 draw splits that the development never used
(\cref{sec:s-development}). On them FedECS-LT reaches \R{fresh/cifar10/FedECS-LT/bal} on CIFAR-10-LT, against
\R{main/cifar10/FedECS-LT/bal} on seeds 3--5, and its margins over the strongest of GeFL-F, FedDF and Balanced
Softmax are \R{fresh/cifar10/margin} and \R{fresh/cifar100/margin} points with heads as trained for the others, and
\R{fresh/cifar10/er_margin} and \R{fresh/cifar100/er_margin} with the readout for all (Holm-corrected
$p=\R{fresh/cifar10/er_strongest_p}$ and $\R{fresh/cifar100/er_strongest_p}$). The margins on new splits are like
those on the development splits, so they show no sign that choosing the settings on seeds 3--5 inflated them.

\paragraph{The pooled-data ceiling} The same extractor and heads trained on all training images in one place reach
\R{main/cifar10/Centralised (pooled data)/bal} on CIFAR-10-LT and \R{main/cifar100/Centralised (pooled data)/bal} on
CIFAR-100-LT, and \R{main/cifar10/Centralised (pooled data)/er_bal} and
\R{main/cifar100/Centralised (pooled data)/er_bal} with the readout. This is not a federated method but the ceiling of
the design: FedECS-LT, at \R{main/cifar10/FedECS-LT/bal} and \R{main/cifar100/FedECS-LT/bal}, is several points below
it, so federation still costs accuracy; and even the ceiling is low, which suggests that the low absolute numbers come
mostly from the small extractor and the long tail.

\paragraph{What it shows} The measured statement is narrow: the full method, with its readout, is above every
published method of this setting as that method was published, by a large and significant margin on both benchmarks.
The heads as trained are also ahead, by less, and the CIFAR-10-LT part of that is not resolved by three seeds.

\paragraph{What it does not show} It does not show that the components before the readout are what helps. Every
other method in this table lacks the readout, and the readout is a known kind of classifier (\cref{sec:s-readout})
that can be added to most of them. The comparison that answers this follows in \cref{sec:s-same-readout}.

\subsection{The other model-heterogeneous methods}

\paragraph{The question} These methods share prototypes, a generator of embeddings or a shared sub-network; the
question is whether any of them does better than the generator-based ones under a long tail.

\paragraph{The answer} None does. In \cref{tab:s-main} FedProto, FedTGP, FedGen, FedGH, LG-FedAvg and FedMRL are close
to or below Local. The paper notes that the methods exchanging prototypes or shared sub-networks (all but FedGen) give
an architecture no information about the classes its own clients lack. A plausible reading is that a prototype of
class 9 is only useful to a head that already predicts class 9. FedProto, FedTGP and FedGen also decline during
training. They are best in the first rounds and lose \R{main/cifar10/FedProto/gefl_drop},
\R{main/cifar10/FedTGP/gefl_drop} and \R{main/cifar10/FedGen/gefl_drop} points by the last round on CIFAR-10-LT. The
heads of FedECS-LT decline too, by \R{main/cifar10/FedECS-LT (heads only)/gefl_drop} points on CIFAR-10-LT. The paper's
appendix gives every method's best round, the measure GeFL reports; the readout is computed once, after the last round,
so it has no best round.

\paragraph{GeFL's grouped baselines} On CIFAR-10-LT the baselines of GeFL's own comparison were also run. With one
client per architecture, FedAvg, FedProx and FedALA within an architecture have nothing to average and reach
\R{main/cifar10/FedAvg/bal}, \R{main/cifar10/FedProx/bal} and \R{main/cifar10/FedALA/bal}, the level of local
training on the shared extractor; CCVR and FedETF reach \R{main/cifar10/CCVR/bal} and \R{main/cifar10/FedETF/bal}.
AvgKD falls to \R{main/cifar10/AvgKD/bal}, chance level, with a worst-class recall of \R{main/cifar10/AvgKD/worst}:
its models predict one class. Its targets are a softmax of the label plus the other nine architectures' predicted
probabilities, exactly as in GeFL's code; a plausible reading, not tested, is that under label skew the nine biased
predictions outweigh the label and every model drifts to the same class.

\paragraph{What it does not show} These methods run as ports onto the frozen extractor with settings from their
library, meant for end-to-end training (\cref{ch:design}, threats). Their low numbers may partly be the port. The paper
did not run a check of the ports.

\paragraph{The numbers} FedECS-LT reaches \R{main/cifar10/FedECS-LT/local} on CIFAR-10-LT and
\R{main/cifar100/FedECS-LT/local} on CIFAR-100-LT, and its heads as trained \R{main/cifar10/FedECS-LT (heads only)/local}
and \R{main/cifar100/FedECS-LT (heads only)/local} (\cref{tab:s-local}). GeFL-F reaches \R{main/cifar10/GeFL-F/local}
on CIFAR-10-LT and Local \R{main/cifar10/Local/local}. FedECS-LT is below \R{main/cifar10/local_n_above} of the

\R{main/cifar100/local_n_other} on CIFAR-100-LT. The readout lowers the measure by about nine points on CIFAR-10-LT.

\paragraph{Why} A client whose test images are mostly class 0 is best served by a last layer that favours class 0.
The readout's equal class priors remove exactly that preference, which explains its measured effect; logit
adjustment in the real phase may do the same to the heads, but its share is not measured. A deployment that cares
about each client's own accuracy could add each client's own log-prior back to the readout's biases at prediction
time, the reverse of post-hoc logit adjustment (\cref{sec:s-lastlayer}); the paper did not test this.

\subsection{The same readout for every method}\label{sec:s-same-readout}

\paragraph{The question} RQ3 asks how much of FedECS-LT's margin remains when every method of the GeFL-F pipeline gets
the same readout. Its answer decides how the paper's claim must be worded.

\paragraph{Why this is the fair comparison of the bodies} With the readout, every last layer is replaced by the same
estimator, computed from the same kind of pooled statistics, through the same procedure. What still differs between two
methods is the body that produced the embeddings. So the difference with the readout measures what FedECS-LT's
other components (the anchored generator, the moment correction, consensus soft labels, logit adjustment and ten
synthetic epochs) do to the bodies, read through one linear classifier. The comparison without the readout measures
the methods as published. A reader needs both: the first says whether FedECS-LT beats what exists; the second says
whether its parts beyond a known classifier contribute.

\paragraph{The numbers} With the readout (\cref{tab:s-er,tab:s-er-paired}) FedECS-LT is \R{main/cifar10/er_diff/GeFL-F}

\R{main/cifar100/er_ci/GeFL-F} on CIFAR-100-LT. Against the strongest other method with the readout,
\R{main/cifar10/er_strongest} on CIFAR-10-LT and \R{main/cifar100/er_strongest} on CIFAR-100-LT, the margins are
\R{main/cifar10/er_margin} \R{main/cifar10/er_strongest_ci} and \R{main/cifar100/er_margin}

($p=\R{main/cifar100/er_p/GeFL-F}$ and $\R{main/cifar100/er_p/FedDF}$).

\paragraph{What it shows} Three things. First, the readout raises every method of the pipeline by a large amount:
Balanced Softmax gains \R{main/cifar10/Balanced Softmax/gain_er} points on CIFAR-10-LT, GeFL-F
\R{main/cifar10/GeFL-F/gain_er}. Second, once every method has it, the methods are close. Third, Balanced Softmax with
the readout reaches \R{main/cifar10/Balanced Softmax/er_bal} on CIFAR-10-LT. With one client per architecture, Balanced
Softmax trains every head on its own client's data with no generator and no exchange after Stage I. So a body trained alone, plus the readout computed over all clients, leaves the full method a lead of only
\R{main/cifar10/er_diff/Balanced Softmax} points. Most of the margin of \cref{sec:s-main} is the readout's.

\paragraph{What it does not show} It does not show that the other components do nothing. Margins of
\R{main/cifar10/er_margin} and \R{main/cifar100/er_margin} points exceed the noise floor of \R{repeat/er/floor}, but
their Holm-corrected tests are not significant, so with three seeds they are neither shown nor ruled out. It also does
not show the components' effect one by one, which is the subject of the ablations (\cref{sec:s-ablations}). And
because the readout is one fixed linear classifier, a body that is better for another last layer would not show it
here.

the job the generator does when models differ. FedECS-LT also trains its head on frozen features of unaugmented
images, while the baselines train the whole network end to end on augmented images (\cref{ch:design};
\cref{fig:s-shared-rounds} shows the round course). For the readout: with one shared model the head is averaged over
the 8 clients of every round, so its last layer is not fitted to one client's prior, the failure the readout repairs;
and a linear discriminant on embeddings that are not Gaussian can be worse than a trained last layer.

\paragraph{The published numbers} The baselines run in their authors' library on the paper's splits and report the
model after the last round. Against FedYoYo's own evaluation in the same protocol, most of them are within about three
points of the published values; that evaluation trains 300 rounds, the paper's 200. Three (CReFF, Fed-GraB, FedLC) are
further below; they run with the library's common settings, which differ from those of their own papers, and were not
tuned.

\paragraph{What it shows} FedECS-LT is a method for clients with different models. When the clients can share one
model, the long-tail methods FedYoYo, FedLoGe and CCVR have far higher means, and plain FedAvg is not shown to be worse.

\subsection{The ablations}\label{sec:s-ablations}

Two groups of runs ask which changes carry the effect: variants of GeFL-F and FedDF that add one change at a time,
and FedECS-LT with one component removed. Both use the splits and seeds of the main comparison, so every difference is
paired.

\paragraph{The decay diagnosis} GeFL-F with decoupled weight decay (AdamW) and nothing else changed is the direct test
of \cref{sec:s-decay}. On CIFAR-10-LT it raises GeFL-F by \R{abl/cifar10/GeFL-F + AdamW/paired} points
($p=\R{abl/cifar10/GeFL-F + AdamW/p}$, uncorrected, at the noise floor of \R{repeat/bal/floor}), its tail recall by
\R{abl/cifar10/GeFL-F + AdamW/tail_paired} ($p=\R{abl/cifar10/GeFL-F + AdamW/tail_p}$), and its accuracy with the
readout by \R{abl/cifar10/GeFL-F + AdamW/er_paired} ($p=\R{abl/cifar10/GeFL-F + AdamW/er_p}$). Its three CIFAR-100-LT
runs ran out of host memory. So the shrinkage of the class rows has a measured cost, but a small one: decoupling the
decay recovers little of GeFL-F's \R{main/cifar10/diff/GeFL-F}-point gap to FedECS-LT. AdamW changes the decay of every
parameter, not only the rows, so even this small gain is consistent with the diagnosis rather than a proof of it.

\paragraph{The longer synthetic phase} GeFL-F and FedDF with ten synthetic epochs per round, as FedECS-LT, change by
\R{abl/cifar10/GeFL-F (10 synthetic epochs)/paired} and \R{abl/cifar10/FedDF (10 synthetic epochs)/paired} points on
CIFAR-10-LT ($p=\R{abl/cifar10/GeFL-F (10 synthetic epochs)/p}$ and $\R{abl/cifar10/FedDF (10 synthetic epochs)/p}$):
more epochs on label-free features make them worse, which is consistent with the label-free lemma of
\cref{sec:s-spread}. FedECS-LT's
heads as trained stay \R{abl/cifar10/heads_vs/GeFL-F (10 synthetic epochs)} and
\R{abl/cifar10/heads_vs/FedDF (10 synthetic epochs)} points ahead of them (CIFAR-100-LT:
\R{abl/cifar100/heads_vs/GeFL-F (10 synthetic epochs)} and \R{abl/cifar100/heads_vs/FedDF (10 synthetic epochs)}). The
lead of the heads is therefore not the extra computation.

\paragraph{Logit adjustment} GeFL-F with decoupled decay and logit adjustment recovers
\R{abl/cifar10/GeFL-F + LA/gain} points on CIFAR-10-LT, and FedECS-LT's heads add \R{abl/cifar10/rest} points on top,
before the readout. Replacing GeFL-F's generator by class Gaussians from the exact class statistics, with logit
adjustment, raises it by \R{abl/cifar10/GeFL-F w/ Gaussian generator + LA/gain} points as trained and by
\R{abl/cifar10/GeFL-F w/ Gaussian generator + LA/er_gain} with the readout: features that carry their label, even from
a simple sampler, teach the heads far more than GeFL-F's trained generator does. A reviewer reading this row will ask why
FedECS-LT keeps a trained generator; the separate note on the Gaussian sampler (\texttt{docs/study/gaussian\_sampler.pdf})
has the numbers for that discussion.

\paragraph{The components of FedECS-LT} Removing one component at a time and comparing with the full method, with the
readout, on the same seeds: the largest loss on both benchmarks comes from ``\R{comp/cifar10/largest}'',
\R{comp/cifar10/largest_drop} points on CIFAR-10-LT (Holm-corrected $p=\R{comp/cifar10/w/o consensus soft labels/er_p}$)
and \R{comp/cifar100/largest_drop} on CIFAR-100-LT ($p=\R{comp/cifar100/w/o consensus soft labels/er_p}$). It is the
only change that is both above the noise floor and significant after Holm's correction over the \R{comp/n_variants}
changes. The other components show no resolved effect beyond the readout:
\begin{itemize}
\item the full method minus its variant without the moment correction is \R{comp/cifar10/w/o moment correction/er_drop}
points on CIFAR-10-LT ($p=\R{comp/cifar10/w/o moment correction/er_p}$): no benefit;
\item class rows with CCWA instead of the mean anchor cost \R{comp/cifar10/w/ class rows (CCWA)/er_drop} points
($p=\R{comp/cifar10/w/ class rows (CCWA)/er_p}$), and with flat averaging
\R{comp/cifar10/w/ class rows (flat averaging)/er_drop} ($p=\R{comp/cifar10/w/ class rows (flat averaging)/er_p}$);
\item one synthetic epoch instead of ten changes it by \R{comp/cifar10/w/ one synthetic epoch/er_drop}, below the floor;
\item without logit adjustment the heads as trained lose \R{comp/cifar10/w/o logit adjustment/drop} points, but
the change with the readout is \R{comp/cifar10/w/o logit adjustment/er_drop}, from one seed.
\end{itemize}
On CIFAR-100-LT \R{comp/cifar100/n_one_seed} of the \R{comp/n_variants} changes have one seed only, because their runs
ran out of host memory. The answer to RQ5 is therefore: of everything FedECS-LT adds before the readout, the consensus
soft labels are the one part with a measured effect beyond it.

\subsection{The experiments still pending}\label{sec:s-pending}

\paragraph{Several clients per architecture} The paper's 50-client setting has five clients per architecture, so heads
of one architecture are averaged in Stage III. The question is whether the readout's dominance is an artefact of one
client per architecture. With averaging, Balanced Softmax's heads would no longer be purely local, and their last
layers would no longer fit one client's prior, so the readout should matter less. The paper is supported if FedECS-LT
stays ahead of every method with the readout; it is weakened if the margins fall to zero. The other methods' 50-client
runs have returned, but FedECS-LT, GeFL-F, FedDF and Balanced Softmax ran out of host memory on both benchmarks, so the
paper shows no 50-client table yet.
\numelse{k50/cifar10/margin}{\numelse{k50/cifar100/er_margin}{\emph{Result:} with 50 clients FedECS-LT is
\R{k50/cifar10/margin} and \R{k50/cifar100/margin} points ahead of the strongest other method with heads as trained,
and \R{k50/cifar10/er_margin} and \R{k50/cifar100/er_margin} with the readout.}{}}{}

\paragraph{Other settings} Sweeps over the label skew ($\alpha=0.1$ and $1.0$), the number of clients (50 and 100)
and the imbalance ratio (50 and 10) on CIFAR-10-LT test whether the result depends on the main setting's values. Their
runs are pending, and the paper's text for them is not yet written. GTSRB, CIFAR-100-LT with one shared model on seed 5,
and the shared-model ratios 50 and 10 are also pending.
