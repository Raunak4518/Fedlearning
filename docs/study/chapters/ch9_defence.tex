\section{Defending the paper}\label{ch:defence}

Three internal reviews of the paper raised the objections below. Each is stated as a reviewer would put it, followed
by the honest answer, the evidence, and the experiment that would settle it with its status.

\subsection*{1. ``Isn't the whole gain just the readout?''}

\paragraph{Answer} Mostly, yes, and the paper says so. With heads as trained for the others, FedECS-LT leads the
strongest other method by \R{main/cifar10/margin} and \R{main/cifar100/margin} points. With the readout for every
method of the GeFL-F pipeline it leads by \R{main/cifar10/er_margin} \R{main/cifar10/er_strongest_ci} and
\R{main/cifar100/er_margin} \R{main/cifar100/er_strongest_ci}, not significant after Holm's correction
($p=\R{main/cifar10/er_strongest_p}$ and $\R{main/cifar100/er_strongest_p}$), and by at most \R{nat/er_margin_hi} on the
natural datasets (\cref{sec:s-same-readout,sec:s-natural}). Balanced Softmax, which has no generator, leaves
FedECS-LT a lead of only \R{main/cifar10/er_diff/Balanced Softmax} points on CIFAR-10-LT once it has the readout.

\paragraph{Why the readout is still part of the method} The readout follows from the diagnosis: \cref{sec:s-lastlayer}
shows that no per-client correction of the loss can give a last layer information about absent classes, and the
readout is the repair that does. The paper therefore defines FedECS-LT as ending with it, credits the family it
belongs to (Fed3R, FedNCM, FedCOF), and reports every method of the pipeline with it added, so the reader can see
exactly how much is the readout's. What the paper must not claim is that the components before the readout have a
demonstrated effect beyond it.

\paragraph{Settled by} The component ablation (RQ5) and the variants of GeFL-F, both pending (\cref{sec:s-pending}).

\subsection*{2. ``The name says exact class statistics, but half the components do not use them''}

\paragraph{Answer} Correct, and the paper says which do (\cref{sec:s-recipe}): the anchored generator (or CCWA), the
moment correction and the readout. Logit adjustment, consensus soft labels and ten synthetic epochs do not. The name
describes the first group, which includes the readout that carries most of the effect.

\paragraph{Settled by} No run; it is a matter of wording, and the abstract separates the two groups.

\subsection*{3. ``Is the weight-decay diagnosis causal?''}

\paragraph{Answer} Not shown. What is measured is that the rare classes' rows are several times shorter
(\cref{fig:s-rows}), as the lemmas predict: class 0 of the running example, held by every client, has no non-holder to
shrink it, while class 9 has two. Against a causal reading: GeFL-F's generated features are recognised at
\R{main/cifar10/GeFL-F/fidelity_all} on CIFAR-10-LT, \R{main/cifar10/GeFL-F/fidelity_head} on the head classes and
\R{main/cifar10/GeFL-F/fidelity_tail} on the tail classes, and at chance on CIFAR-100-LT
(\R{main/cifar100/GeFL-F/fidelity_all}). If the shrinkage were what loses the rare classes, the head classes, whose rows
are long, would be recognised; they are not. The row lengths also do not fall monotonically with class size, and the
step-count heuristic $\kappa_c$ was not checked against the rows. The paper calls the shrinkage a measured property
of the generator, not a demonstrated cause.

\paragraph{Settled by} GeFL-F with decoupled weight decay alone (AdamW), pending. If it raises GeFL-F's tail recall
above the noise floor and significantly, and its rare rows stop shrinking, the diagnosis passes a direct test; if not,
the paper should say the shrinkage has no measured consequence for accuracy.

\subsection*{4. ``Do the other diagnoses matter: label-free generated features, and the moment correction?''}

\paragraph{Answer} The label-free lemma is exact, but its size in the pipeline is not settled: FedDF, on GeFL-F's own
generator, loses less logit spread than FedECS-LT (\cref{sec:s-spread}). The moment correction brings the generated
spreads to \R{main/mc_spread_lo} to \R{main/mc_spread_hi} times the exact ones, but its fidelity changes on the tail
classes and on average are within one standard deviation over the seeds (\cref{sec:s-mc}). Its value for accuracy is
unmeasured.

\paragraph{Settled by} The component ablation (``w/o moment correction'', ``w/o consensus soft labels''), pending.

\subsection*{5. ``Were the settings tuned on the test sets?''}

\paragraph{Answer} The development used the same splits (seeds 3--5) and the same test sets as the reported runs,
comparing about 36 configurations on CIFAR-10-LT and 17 on CIFAR-100-LT; the baselines were not tuned
(\cref{sec:s-development}). The CIFAR margins may therefore favour FedECS-LT. Two things limit the damage. The six
naturally imbalanced datasets were never used in development, and there the pattern repeats: large margins with heads
as trained for the others, small ones with the readout (\cref{sec:s-natural}). And every setting is the same on every
dataset; nothing was set per dataset.

\paragraph{Settled by} The run on new splits, seeds 6--8, pending.

\subsection*{6. ``Is the heads' advantage just more computation and ensemble distillation?''}

\paragraph{Answer} It may be. FedECS-LT trains ten synthetic epochs per round against one for GeFL-F and FedDF, with
consensus soft labels, which are a form of ensemble distillation; its training costs about two and a half times the
GFLOPs per round of GeFL-F (\cref{tab:s-cost}). The margin of FedECS-LT's heads as trained over FedDF,
\R{main/cifar10/heads_margin} on CIFAR-10-LT, is not significant ($p=\R{main/cifar10/heads_strongest_p}$) and has not
been separated from this extra computation.

\paragraph{Settled by} GeFL-F and FedDF with ten synthetic epochs, and FedDF with logit adjustment, pending.

\subsection*{7. ``With one client per architecture nothing is averaged; does the result hold with several?''}

\paragraph{Answer} Unknown. In every reported result each architecture has one client, so Stage III averages nothing,
and the per-client losses become purely local training. That is the case in which the readout should help most,
because each last layer then fits one client's prior. With several clients per architecture, heads are averaged and
the readout may matter less.

\paragraph{Settled by} The 50-client runs (five per architecture), pending.

\subsection*{8. ``If every client runs every head, why have different models at all?''}

\paragraph{Answer} The clients still \emph{train} different networks, but each must be able to evaluate all of them
(\cref{ch:limits}). The paper states this as an assumption of its setting. It weakens the hardware motivation of model
heterogeneity, and the answer should concede that.

\paragraph{Settled by} Not by a run. A variant in which a client skips the heads it cannot run, and the readout's sums
omit it, is not implemented.

\subsection*{9. ``Why is it worse than FedAvg with one shared model?''}

\paragraph{Answer} First correct the premise: on CIFAR-10-LT, paired over the two seeds both have, FedECS-LT minus FedAvg is \R{t2/cifar10-r100/diff/FedAvg} \R{t2/cifar10-r100/ci/FedAvg} points, which is unresolved; the
methods that are far ahead are FedYoYo, FedLoGe and CCVR (\cref{sec:s-shared}). The honest conclusion is that
FedECS-LT is a method for clients with different models, and that in this protocol it is behind the strongest long-tail
methods built for it, FedYoYo, FedLoGe and CCVR. Why, the paper did not test. Possible reasons are that with one shared model FedAvg already carries
every client's knowledge into every model, that FedECS-LT trains its head on frozen features of unaugmented images
while the baselines train end to end with augmentation, and, for the readout's
\R{t2/cifar10-r100/FedECS-LT/gain_er}-point effect, that the shared head's last layer is not fitted to one client's
prior.

\paragraph{Settled by} Not planned. A run that trains FedECS-LT's extractor for all 200 rounds would test the
extractor part of the second reason\numelse{t2/cifar10-r100/fe200_diff}{; such a run changes the heads by
\R{t2/cifar10-r100/fe200_diff} points}{}.

\subsection*{10. ``Is a small gain worth \R{cost/cifar10/comm_ratio_bsm} times the communication?''}

\paragraph{Answer} Against Balanced Softmax with the readout, on CIFAR-10-LT, the measured gain is
\R{main/cifar10/er_diff/Balanced Softmax} points, not significant, for \R{cost/cifar10/comm_ratio_bsm} times the
communication and \R{cost/cifar10/time_ratio_bsm} times the time. On this evidence, a practitioner who only needs
balanced accuracy in this setting would choose per-client Balanced Softmax plus the exact readout, which needs no
generator. FedECS-LT's extra cost is justified only if the pending ablations show that its components before the
readout add accuracy, or in settings, such as several clients per architecture, where they may add more. Against
GeFL-F, the base it extends, the overhead is \R{cost/cifar10/comm_ratio} times the communication and
\R{cost/cifar10/time_ratio} times the time.

\subsection*{11. ``What do the released statistics leak?''}

\paragraph{Answer} The class means and spreads of the federation, which for a singly held or small class reveal much
about one client's images (\cref{sec:s-privacy}). The paper did not test an inversion of the means and has no
differential privacy, so it claims nothing beyond secure aggregation against an honest-but-curious server.

\paragraph{Settled by} An inversion test on the smallest classes, or a differentially private release with its
accuracy cost; neither is planned.

\subsection*{12. ``Why are the absolute accuracies so low?''}

\paragraph{Answer} Because the setting is GeFL-F's: a two-convolution extractor with 3 and 10 channels, frozen after
Stage I, and small heads, under a long tail with ten clients. GeFL's authors attribute GeFL-F's lower accuracy to
this extractor and these heads. In GeFL's own balanced setting the paper's earlier implementation of GeFL-F reaches
\R{repro/cifar10/GeFL-F/final}\% on CIFAR-10, against about \R{repro/cifar10/published}\% in GeFL's paper, and the
Flower implementation of the reported runs was checked against that earlier one (\cref{ch:limits}). A stronger or
pre-trained extractor was not studied.

\paragraph{Settled by} The reference trained on all data pooled, a notebook of its own and pending, gives the ceiling
of this extractor and these heads.

\subsection*{13. ``How is this different from FedCOF, Fed3R or CCVR?''}

\paragraph{Answer} The readout belongs to the same family as Fed3R (ridge regression), FedNCM (nearest class means) and
FedCOF (an LDA-type classifier); these also note that their sums do not depend on the partition. The paper does not
claim the readout or partition invariance as new. Its contributions are elsewhere: the diagnosis of which
class-specific quantities of generator-based model-heterogeneous learning fail under a long tail, and where the readout
is applied, per architecture, on bodies trained in the federation rather than pre-trained, with statistics from every
client, including clients of other architectures. CCVR samples features from Gaussians with the pooled class moments
to retrain a shared classifier; the moment correction instead maps a learned generator's samples onto those moments.

\paragraph{Settled by} The pending ``w/ Gaussian sampler'' component run compares FedECS-LT's generator with a
CCVR-style sampler directly.

\subsection*{14. ``Are the model-heterogeneous baselines weakened by the port?''}

\paragraph{Answer} Possibly. FedProto, FedTGP, FedGen, FedGH, LG-FedAvg and FedMRL run on the frozen extractor with
HtFLlib's settings, which were meant for end-to-end training, and the paper did not tune them or check the port
against their published numbers. It discloses this as a threat to validity. The comparison that decides the paper's
claims is with GeFL-F, FedDF and the per-client losses, which run in the same pipeline as FedECS-LT.

\paragraph{Settled by} A port check against the methods' published settings; not planned.

\subsection*{15. ``Why only three seeds?''}

\paragraph{Answer} Compute: the study has several hundred runs, and a run of a generator-based method takes about two
hours on a shared Kaggle GPU (\cref{tab:s-cost}). Three seeds give little power. A sign-flip permutation test cannot go
below $p=0.25$ with three pairs, so every significant result rests on the $t$-test's normality assumption. Margins of
one to two points with the readout exceed the noise floor of \R{repeat/er/floor} but fail Holm's correction
(\cref{sec:s-stats-design}). The paper therefore reports intervals, applies Holm's correction, calls such differences
unresolved rather than absent, and adds three new seeds for the main comparison (pending).
