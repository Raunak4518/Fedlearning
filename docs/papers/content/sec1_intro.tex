\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Federated learning (FL) trains a model on data that stay with their owners~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg,kairouz2021advances}.
Two properties of real deployments make it hard. The participants run different networks on different hardware, so
their parameters cannot be averaged. Their label distributions differ and follow a long tail, so the rarest classes
are held by few participants and in small numbers. Each property has its own line of work: model-heterogeneous
FL~\cite{tan2022fedproto,zhang2024fedtgp,zhu2021fedgen,yi2023fedgh,yi2024fedmrl,kang2025gefl} lets clients with
different architectures share knowledge through prototypes, logits, generators or shared sub-networks, and
federated long-tailed learning~\cite{shang2022creff,xiao2023fedgrab,xiao2024fedloge,yan2025fedyoyo,huang2024rucr}
corrects the bias that a skewed, long-tailed label distribution induces in a single shared model. The two are rarely
studied together: model-heterogeneous methods are evaluated without a global long tail~\cite{zhang2025htfllib}, and
federated long-tail methods assume one shared model.

We study generator-based model-heterogeneous FL, in the form of GeFL-F~\cite{kang2025gefl}. Its clients first train a
small feature extractor by federated averaging (FedAvg) and freeze it; they then train a conditional feature generator
by FedAvg; finally every client trains a head of its own architecture on generated features and on its own real
features. The generator is the only channel through which an architecture learns about classes its clients lack.
GeFL-F was evaluated on balanced clients. Under long-tailed label skew we find that it loses most of its accuracy on
the rare classes, and we ask where.

\paragraph{Where the rare classes are lost} Because a client partition is a pure label shift, federation cannot
bias class-conditional quantities by itself (\cref{sec:setting}). We examine how it can still lose rare classes:
through the optimiser, through parameters tied to a client's label prior, through the synthetic data and through
sample size. We locate three such losses, one through each of the first three routes (\cref{sec:analysis}).
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The generator.} With Adam~\cite{kingma2015adam} and coupled weight decay, a client that lacks a class moves
the generator's class row, its only class-specific parameter, toward zero by about one learning rate per step, while
the clients that hold the class barely grow it. Flat averaging then shrinks the rare classes' rows round after round.
\item \emph{The last layer.} Each head is trained on one client's real data, so its last layer fits that client's
label prior. Per-client logit corrections (logit adjustment~\cite{menon2021la}, FedLC~\cite{zhang2022fedlc},
Balanced Softmax~\cite{ren2020bsm} and, up to a step size, FedRS~\cite{li2021fedrs}) change only the initialisation
of this layer and bring in no information about the classes the client lacks.
\item \emph{The synthetic data.} When generated features do not carry their label, the synthetic phase of head
training penalises the spread of the logits instead of teaching the missing classes.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Our method} \ours (Exact Class Statistics for Long-Tailed FL) replaces each estimate that fails by a class
statistic that secure aggregation~\cite{bonawitz2017secagg,bell2020secaggplus} computes exactly: the per-class
counts, feature sums and sums of squared feature norms over all clients. The generator is anchored to the class means
and has no class row for the decay to shrink; when the classes are poorly separated, a gate computed from the same
statistics falls back to class rows averaged over their holders only (class-count weighted averaging, CCWA).
Generated features are rescaled to the exact class means and spreads, and heads are trained with logit adjustment and
with consensus soft labels, the averaged prediction of all architectures. As its last step, \ours sets every
architecture's last layer to the exact readout, a class-balanced linear discriminant computed from aggregated sums of
its embeddings. None of these statistics is new on its own: aggregated class
statistics underlie classifier calibration~\cite{luo2021ccvr}, closed-form federated
classifiers~\cite{fani2024fed3r,legate2023fedncm,goswami2025fedcof}, including an LDA-type classifier from class
statistics~\cite{goswami2025fedcof}, and prototype methods~\cite{tan2022fedproto}, and statistics-conditioned
generators have been proposed for heterogeneous clients~\cite{niu2026fedvtc}. What we add is the diagnosis of
\emph{which} parameters fail under a long tail and the use of exact statistics at exactly those places, including a
readout per architecture that needs statistics pooled across architectures.

\paragraph{Evaluation} We re-implemented the pipeline and every comparison of \cref{sec:results} in the Flower framework~\cite{beutel2020flower},
with the published methods taken from their authors' or library code (HtFLlib~\cite{zhang2025htfllib},
FedLTLib~\cite{lin2026fedltlib}, whose code is LongTailLib). We evaluate in two protocols: the model-heterogeneous setting of
GeFL-F (ten client architectures; long-tailed \R{main/datasets} and, with natural class imbalance,
\R{nat/datasets}; \R{main/nbaselines_w} other methods) and the standard federated long-tailed protocol with one
shared ResNet-8 (long-tailed \R{t2/datasets} at imbalance ratio \R{t2/ratios}; \R{t2/nbaselines_w} other methods). Every
result is a mean over seeds 3--5 (fewer where a table says so), with paired tests and a Holm correction, and every
metric is logged per class.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A long-tail evaluation of generator-based model-heterogeneous FL and a measured, stage-by-stage diagnosis of
where it loses rare classes, supported by elementary lemmas (\cref{sec:analysis}).
\item \ours, which uses exact, securely aggregated class statistics in the generator, the heads and the readout
(\cref{sec:method}).
\item A comparison against \R{count/methods_w} published methods in two protocols: \R{main/nbaselines_w} on
\R{count/datasets_w} datasets with different client models, and \R{t2/nbaselines_w} on two benchmarks with one shared
model (in the authors' LongTailLib code), each protocol on the same client splits, with paired statistics, per-class
metrics and the cost of every method (\cref{sec:results}). Every method of the GeFL-F pipeline is also evaluated with
the readout of \ours added, which separates the readout's gain from that of the rest of the method. \claim{With
different client models, \ours has the highest mean balanced accuracy on \R{main/nbest_of} long-tailed benchmarks and
\R{nat/nbest_of} naturally imbalanced datasets, and most of its margin comes from the readout: with the same readout
for every method of the GeFL-F pipeline, its lead of \R{main/er_margin_lo} to \R{main/er_margin_hi} points over the
strongest other method on the long-tailed benchmarks is not significant with three seeds. With one shared model, the strongest long-tail methods are better.}
\end{enumerate}
The code, every log and every figure's data are released (\cref{sec:availability}).
