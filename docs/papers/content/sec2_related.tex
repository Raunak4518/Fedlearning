\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Model-heterogeneous federated learning.} When clients run different architectures, their parameters cannot
be averaged and knowledge is exchanged in another form. Distillation methods exchange predictions on public or
synthetic inputs (FedMD~\cite{li2019fedmd}, FedDF~\cite{lin2020feddf}). Prototype methods exchange class means of
embeddings (FedProto~\cite{tan2022fedproto}) or learn global prototypes on the server (FedTGP~\cite{zhang2024fedtgp}).
Other methods share a part of the network: the head (LG-FedAvg~\cite{liang2020lgfedavg}, FedGH~\cite{yi2023fedgh}) or a
small homogeneous model next to each client's own (FedMRL~\cite{yi2024fedmrl}). Generator-based methods train a
generator against the ensemble of client heads on the server (FedGen~\cite{zhu2021fedgen},
FedFTG~\cite{zhang2022fedftg}), train generators from feature-distribution statistics that the clients send to the
server (FedVTC~\cite{niu2026fedvtc}, which targets generalisation to unseen data rather than long-tailed classes), or
train a conditional
generator on the clients by FedAvg (GeFL, GeFL-F~\cite{kang2025gefl}). HtFLlib~\cite{zhang2025htfllib} benchmarks these
methods under label skew but not under a long-tailed global distribution. \ours keeps GeFL-F's three-stage design and
changes how its class-specific quantities are estimated.

\paragraph{Federated learning under label skew and long tails.} With one shared model, the classifier is the most
biased part under label skew. Classifier calibration re-trains it on virtual features drawn from aggregated class
statistics (CCVR~\cite{luo2021ccvr}) or on federated features learned on the server (CReFF~\cite{shang2022creff});
closed-form classifiers are computed from aggregated sums (Fed3R~\cite{fani2024fed3r}, a ridge-regression classifier,
and FedNCM~\cite{legate2023fedncm}, nearest class means); our readout is a class-balanced linear discriminant of the same
kind, computed for each architecture's embeddings; FedETF fixes the classifier to a simplex equiangular tight
frame~\cite{li2023fedetf}. Loss corrections use each client's label frequencies (FedLC~\cite{zhang2022fedlc},
FedRS~\cite{li2021fedrs}, FedRoD~\cite{chen2022fedrod}), and long-tail methods re-balance gradients
(Fed-GraB~\cite{xiao2023fedgrab}), decouple a generic and a local head (FedLoGe~\cite{xiao2024fedloge}), combine prototype
alignment with classifier rectification (RUCR~\cite{huang2024rucr}) or distil between augmented views of local data
(FedYoYo~\cite{yan2025fedyoyo}). FedLTLib~\cite{lin2026fedltlib} benchmarks them in one protocol, which we follow for
the one-shared-model setting. Class-specific weights are also known to degrade when clients lack classes
(FedAwS~\cite{yu2020fedaws}, FedRS). These methods assume one architecture; their readout ideas apply to each of our
architectures only if the statistics are pooled across architectures, which \ours does.

\paragraph{Long-tailed learning, optimisers and synthetic data.} \ours builds on logit adjustment~\cite{menon2021la},
effective numbers of samples~\cite{cui2019cb} and the decoupling of representation and
classifier~\cite{kang2020decoupling}. The generator analysis rests on the difference between coupled L2 regularisation
and decoupled weight decay in Adam~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw,kingma2015adam}. Class-conditional generators can collapse
on classes with little data even centrally~\cite{shahbazi2022collapse}, and synthetic training data help only as far
as they carry their labels~\cite{ravuri2019cas}. Generators matched to kernel mean embeddings of the data have been used
for private data release~\cite{harder2021dpmerf}.
