\section{Where generator-based heterogeneous FL loses rare classes}\label{sec:analysis}

This section locates the three losses that \ours repairs. Each comes with a short exact statement (proofs in
\cref{app:proofs}) and a measurement on the GeFL-F runs of \cref{sec:results}.

\subsection{The generator: coupled decay shrinks rare classes' conditioning rows}\label{sec:analysis-gen}

In one round, client $k$ starts from the global row $r_c$, runs local Adam steps and returns $r_{k,c}$. Let
$\Delta_k=\|r_{k,c}\|-\|r_c\|$, let $\bar G_c$ be the mean of $\Delta_k$ over the holders $H_c$ and $\bar D_c$ the mean
of $-\Delta_k$ over the other clients, and $p_c=m_c/K$.

\begin{proposition}[contraction under flat averaging]\label{prop:contraction}
Under $r_c^+=\frac1K\sum_k r_{k,c}$, $\|r_c^+\|\le\|r_c\|+p_c\bar G_c-(1-p_c)\bar D_c$, with equality if and only if
all $r_{k,c}$ are parallel. The row contracts whenever $p_c<\bar D_c/(\bar G_c+\bar D_c)$.
\end{proposition}

The bound only describes the measured changes; the optimiser decides how large $\bar D_c$ is.

\begin{lemma}[coupled decay under Adam]\label{lem:adam}
On a client without class $c$, the gradient of the loss with coupled L2 regularisation $\lambda$ with respect to $r_c$
is exactly $\lambda r_c$. Since Adam is invariant to a positive rescaling of the gradient sequence up to its
$\epsilon$, each step of such a client moves every coordinate of $r_c$ toward zero by about the learning rate $\eta$,
independently of $\lambda$, as long as $\lambda|r_{c,j}|\gg\epsilon$. With $\lambda=0$, or with decoupled weight
decay~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw}, this shrinkage vanishes.
\end{lemma}

A holder's data gradient fits the row and need not increase its norm, so $\bar G_c\ll\bar D_c$ and rare classes' rows
contract even when most clients hold the class; a threshold on $m_c$ alone cannot decide it. Counting Adam steps gives
a predictor that uses only the class counts and the number of local steps $\tau_k$: with $f_{kc}$ the probability that
a batch of client $k$ contains class $c$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa}
\kappa_c=\frac{\sum_{k\in H_c}\tau_k f_{kc}}{\sum_{k\notin H_c}\tau_k},
\end{equation}
and the row drifts toward zero in every coordinate when $\kappa_c<1$. This count of Adam steps is a heuristic, not
a bound: it ignores the moments Adam carries between rounds (\cref{app:proofs}). Centrally the same decay is
harmless, because Adam's second-moment estimate for $r_c$ is dominated by data gradients as long as the class appears
more often than the estimate forgets.

\whenfig{fig_rows}{\claim{\Cref{fig:rows} shows the effect on GeFL-F: after Stage II the conditioning rows of the
rarest classes are several times shorter than those of the most frequent classes.}} Two remedies follow. Decoupling the decay removes the
shrinkage. Averaging each row over its holders only, with weights that grow with the holder's count (class-count
weighted averaging, CCWA), removes the non-holders' term $\bar D_c$ from \cref{prop:contraction} and keeps coupled
decay:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ccwa}
r_c\leftarrow\frac{\sum_k E(n_{kc})\,r_{k,c}}{\sum_k E(n_{kc})},\qquad E(n)=\frac{1-\beta^n}{1-\beta},\quad
\beta=0.999,
\end{equation}
where $E(n)$ is the effective number of samples~\cite{cui2019cb}: zero for a client without the class, about $n$ for
small $n$, and bounded by $1/(1-\beta)$ so that one large client cannot dominate a class. Both sums in \cref{eq:ccwa}
are computed by secure aggregation.

\subsection{The last layer: logit corrections are reparametrisations}\label{sec:analysis-head}

\begin{proposition}[logit corrections are reparametrisations]\label{prop:reparam}
Let a head have a free last-layer bias $b$, trained by SGD without decay on $b$, and let $a_k\in\Rset^C$ be a constant
of client $k$, as in logit adjustment ($a_k=\tau\log\pi_k$)~\cite{menon2021la}, FedLC~\cite{zhang2022fedlc} or Balanced
Softmax~\cite{ren2020bsm}. Training with the loss $\ell(f+a_k,y)$ from $(\theta_0,b_0)$ produces exactly the trajectory
of training with $\ell(f,y)$ from $(\theta_0,b_0+a_k)$: every parameter except $b$ is the same at every step. FedRS,
which scales the logits of absent classes by $\alpha$~\cite{li2021fedrs}, is cross-entropy in rescaled last-layer
variables with a larger learning rate on those rows; the body follows the same trajectory.
\end{proposition}

For a head trained on one client's real data, such a correction is therefore equivalent to a different
initialisation of the last-layer bias. This does not mean the representation is the one plain training from the same
bias would learn: the logits, and hence the gradients reaching the body, differ along the way. What the proposition
does show is that the correction adds no information about the classes the client lacks; its effect on the body is
measured by removing logit adjustment from \ours with and without the readout below\whentab{abl_components}{
(\cref{tab:components})}. The readout itself can be computed exactly.

\begin{proposition}[partition invariance]\label{prop:invariance}
An estimator that is a function of a sum $S=\sum_k\sum_{i\in D_k}T(x_i,y_i)$, with $T$ fixed in advance, takes the same
value for every partition of the pooled data, and equals the centralised estimator.
\end{proposition}

The class-balanced linear discriminant used by \ours (\cref{sec:method-readout}) is such an estimator, and so are
Fed3R~\cite{fani2024fed3r}, FedNCM~\cite{legate2023fedncm} and CCVR~\cite{luo2021ccvr} when computed over all clients.
The last layer then stops being a source of loss relative to the same readout computed centrally on the same
bodies, and the accuracy with this readout compares the bodies. \claim{The readout alone raises GeFL-F's balanced
accuracy by \R{main/cifar10/GeFL-F/gain_er} points on CIFAR-10-LT and \R{main/cifar100/GeFL-F/gain_er} on
CIFAR-100-LT (\cref{tab:readout}).} A head trained on one client is an extreme case of minority
collapse~\cite{fang2021minority}: for the classes the client lacks, the imbalance ratio is infinite.

\subsection{The synthetic data: label-free features penalise the logits' spread}\label{sec:analysis-syn}

\begin{proposition}[label-free synthetic data]\label{prop:variance}
If the synthetic label $y$ is uniform on $\{1,\dots,C\}$ and independent of the generated feature, then for every logit
vector $f$, $\E_y\,\ell(f,y)=\LSE(f)-\bar f=\log C+\KL(u\,\|\,\mathrm{softmax}f)
=\log C+\frac1{2C}\sum_c(f_c-\bar f)^2+O(\|f-\bar f\|^3)$, where $\bar f$ is the mean logit and $u$ the uniform
distribution.
\end{proposition}

When generated features carry little information about their label, the synthetic phase therefore pulls the logits
together instead of teaching the classes a client lacks. Alternating it with the real phase decides what survives:
in an illustrative model with quadratic losses (the heads are nonlinear and trained with cross-entropy) the round map
converges to a point where the synthetic data decide exactly the directions that
the client's real loss leaves free (\cref{app:proofs}), which are the directions of the absent classes. Logit
adjustment keeps the real phase from pushing the absent classes' logits down without bound~\cite{soudry2018implicit}.
