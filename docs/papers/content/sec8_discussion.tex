\section{Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

The threats to the validity of the study design are discussed in \cref{sec:setup-threats}; this section lists the
limits of what the study covers.
\begin{itemize}
\item The extractor is small and frozen, as in GeFL-F, and shared by all clients, so the clients' architectures
differ only in their heads; we did not study pre-trained or larger extractors, or clients with different backbones.
\item No published method addresses both different client models and a long tail, so the comparison is with
long-tail losses applied per client, with FedDF and with model-heterogeneous methods designed for label skew; the
long-tail methods CCVR and FedETF\numelse{main/cifar10/CCVR/bal}{ enter only GeFL's grouped protocol, within
each architecture}{ are not compared with different client models}.
\item The client splits are a pure label shift (\cref{sec:setting}); feature shift and concept shift between clients
are not tested.
\item In the main setting each architecture has one client ($K=10$)\whentab{robust}{; settings with several clients
per architecture are covered only by \cref{tab:robust}}.
\item The released class statistics are not differentially private (\cref{sec:method-cost}); \ours should not be
called privacy-preserving beyond the guarantee of secure aggregation against an honest-but-curious server.
\item \ours costs more than GeFL-F (\cref{tab:cost}): its extra time is mostly in the longer synthetic phase of
Stage III, and its extra communication mostly in the training of the generator, which with its projection of the
class means is larger than GeFL-F's.
\item The absolute accuracy of the main setting is low. GeFL's authors attribute GeFL-F's lower accuracy on CIFAR-10
to its feature extractor and target networks (their term for the heads)~\cite{kang2025gefl}. In GeFL's own setting
(a balanced training set divided evenly among 10 clients) our earlier implementation of GeFL-F reaches
\R{repro/cifar10/GeFL-F/final}\% on CIFAR-10 (\R{repro/cifar10/GeFL-F/best}\% with GeFL's best-round measure;
GeFL's Fig.~4 shows about \R{repro/cifar10/published}\%) and \R{repro/cifar100/GeFL-F/final}\% on CIFAR-100
(\cref{app:protocol}).\whennum{main/cifar100/Centralised (pooled data)/bal}{ The pooled-data reference
(\cref{sec:setup-methods}) reaches \R{main/cifar10/Centralised (pooled data)/bal} on CIFAR-10-LT and
\R{main/cifar100/Centralised (pooled data)/bal} on CIFAR-100-LT (\R{main/cifar10/Centralised (pooled data)/er_bal} and
\R{main/cifar100/Centralised (pooled data)/er_bal} with the exact readout).} These numbers are not comparable with
centralised long-tail results for ResNet-32 and larger networks.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We examined three places where generator-based model-heterogeneous federated learning can lose rare classes:
coupled weight decay under Adam on clients that lack a class, which shrinks the rare classes' rows in GeFL-F's
generator but is not yet shown to cause their loss; a last layer fitted to one client's label prior; and generated
features that do not carry their label. Each is addressed by a component of \ours that uses class statistics computed by secure
aggregation or, for the heads, logit adjustment and consensus soft labels.\numelse{comp/cifar10/largest}{}{ Which of
these components carry the effect beyond the readout is not yet measured.} \claim{\ours, which applies these repairs together and ends with an exact readout of every last layer, improves
balanced accuracy over the published methods when clients have different models, on CIFAR-10-LT mostly through the
recall of the tail classes. Most of the gain is the readout's: added to every method of the GeFL-F pipeline, it
leaves \ours a lead over the strongest other method that three seeds do not resolve. With one model shared by all clients, \R{t2/cifar10-r100/n_above}
other methods have a higher mean on CIFAR-10-LT. \ours also trades accuracy on the clients' own class mixes and, for its
readout and consensus, more communication and the condition that every client can evaluate every head.}
