\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the gains consist of.} A large part of what any method of the GeFL-F family loses under a long tail is
in its last layer, and an exact readout recovers it for every member of the family (\cref{tab:readout}).
\claim{The difference between \ours and the strongest baseline is therefore smaller with the readout than without it;
we report both, and the comparison with the readout is the one that measures the representation.}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The extractor is small and frozen, as in GeFL-F, and shared by all clients, so the clients' architectures
differ only in their heads; we did not study pre-trained or larger extractors, or clients with different backbones.
\item The splits are pure label skew (\cref{sec:setting}); feature and concept shift between clients are not tested.
\item Three seeds per setting give fragile intervals and paired tests; the tests are reported with Holm's correction,
and differences of about a point should be read with that in mind.
\item In the main setting each architecture has one client ($K=10$)\whentab{robust}{; settings with several clients
per architecture are covered only by \cref{tab:robust}}.
\item Secure aggregation hides each client's contribution from an honest-but-curious server, but the released
federation-level sums are not differentially private: for classes with few samples they describe those samples
closely. \ours should not be called privacy-preserving beyond that protocol guarantee.
\item \claim{Some settings were run with fewer than three seeds; their tables say so and carry no interval.}
\item \ours costs more than GeFL-F (\cref{tab:cost}), mainly through the longer synthetic phase, and we did not
compare the methods at matched computation or communication.
\item The gate threshold $F_0$ and the other settings of \cref{tab:hyper} were fixed before these runs and not swept.
\item With one model shared by all clients, end-to-end long-tail methods such as FedYoYo, FedLoGe and CCVR are
clearly stronger (\cref{tab:shared}); \ours is designed for clients with different models and has no advantage
when their models can be averaged.
\item The absolute accuracy of the main setting is low because of GeFL-F's design, a frozen one-block extractor and
small heads, not because of the federation alone: GeFL's own methods reach 52--59\% on CIFAR-10 with balanced data and
full models~\cite{kang2025gefl}\whennum{main/cifar100/Centralised (pooled data)/bal}{, and the same extractor and heads
trained on all the training data pooled reach \R{main/cifar10/Centralised (pooled data)/bal} on CIFAR-10-LT and
\R{main/cifar100/Centralised (pooled data)/bal} on CIFAR-100-LT (\R{main/cifar10/Centralised (pooled data)/er_bal} and
\R{main/cifar100/Centralised (pooled data)/er_bal} with the exact readout), the ceiling of this design}. These numbers
are not comparable with centralised long-tail results for ResNet-32 and larger networks.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Generator-based model-heterogeneous federated learning loses rare classes in three places: the optimiser's treatment
of clients that lack a class, a last layer fitted to one client's prior, and synthetic features that do not carry
their label. Each can be described precisely, and each can be repaired with class statistics that secure aggregation
computes up to its quantisation. \claim{\ours, which applies these repairs together, improves balanced accuracy and in
particular the recall of the rarest classes when clients have different models; with one model shared by all
clients, end-to-end long-tail methods remain stronger.} Its code, logs and every figure's
data are public.
