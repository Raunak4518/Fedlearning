\subsection{Main comparison: different client models}\label{sec:res-main}

\Cref{tab:main} gives the balanced accuracy of every method on the long-tailed benchmarks (\R{main/datasets}): \ours
with its readout, its heads before the readout (heads only), and every other method as published; \cref{tab:paired}

\R{main/nbest_of} benchmarks. Its margin over the strongest other method is \R{main/cifar10/margin} points on
CIFAR-10-LT (over \R{main/cifar10/strongest}) and \R{main/cifar100/margin} on CIFAR-100-LT (over
\R{main/cifar100/strongest}), with Holm-corrected $p=\R{main/cifar10/strongest_p}$ and
$\R{main/cifar100/strongest_p}$.} \claim{Its heads alone are \R{main/cifar10/heads_margin} and
\R{main/cifar100/heads_margin} points ahead of the strongest other method (\R{main/cifar10/heads_strongest} and
\R{main/cifar100/heads_strongest}; $p=\R{main/cifar10/heads_strongest_p}$ and $\R{main/cifar100/heads_strongest_p}$).} The readout accounts
for the rest; whether the rest of the method still helps once every method has the readout is the subject of the next
paragraph. \claim{On CIFAR-100-LT, \R{main/cifar100/FedECS-LT/nzero} classes on average are recognised by none of the
ten client models of \ours (\R{main/cifar100/FedECS-LT (heads only)/nzero} before the readout), against
\R{main/cifar100/strongest_nzero} for \R{main/cifar100/strongest}.} \claim{Against GeFL-F, the method it extends, the
difference is \R{main/cifar10/diff/GeFL-F} points \R{main/cifar10/ci/GeFL-F} on CIFAR-10-LT and
\R{main/cifar100/diff/GeFL-F} \R{main/cifar100/ci/GeFL-F} on CIFAR-100-LT.} The model-heterogeneous methods that
exchange prototypes or shared sub-networks (FedProto, FedTGP, FedGH, LG-FedAvg, FedMRL) were designed for label skew
without a global long tail; \claim{under the long tail their balanced accuracy is close to or below that of Local}, because
nothing in them gives an architecture information about the classes its clients lack. \claim{FedProto, FedTGP and FedGen are best in the first rounds and lose
\R{main/cifar10/FedProto/gefl_drop}, \R{main/cifar10/FedTGP/gefl_drop} and \R{main/cifar10/FedGen/gefl_drop} points of
accuracy by the last round on CIFAR-10-LT (\R{main/cifar100/FedProto/gefl_drop}, \R{main/cifar100/FedTGP/gefl_drop}
and \R{main/cifar100/FedGen/gefl_drop} on CIFAR-100-LT); they are also the weakest on their clients' own class mixes,
on which HtFLlib scores such personal models (\cref{tab:app-local}). The heads of \ours, too, are past their best
round at the last one, by \R{main/cifar10/FedECS-LT (heads only)/gefl_drop} points on CIFAR-10-LT and
\R{main/cifar100/FedECS-LT (heads only)/gefl_drop} on CIFAR-100-LT; \cref{tab:app-gefl} gives every method's best
round, as GeFL reports it (the readout is computed once, after the last round).}
\claim{Balanced accuracy has a price on the clients' own class mixes, and the class-balanced readout raises it. There

\subsection{One shared model}\label{sec:res-shared}

\Cref{tab:shared} gives the results in the FedLTLib protocol. \claim{Here several long-tail methods are
stronger: \R{t2/cifar10-r100/above_list} are above \ours on CIFAR-10-LT and \R{t2/cifar100-r100/above_list} on
CIFAR-100-LT, and \R{t2/cifar10-r100/n_below} and \R{t2/cifar100-r100/n_below} methods are below it. At imbalance
ratio 100, \ours reaches \R{t2/cifar10-r100/FedECS-LT/bal} on CIFAR-10-LT and
\R{t2/cifar100-r100/FedECS-LT/bal} on CIFAR-100-LT, against \R{t2/cifar10-r100/FedAvg/bal} and
\R{t2/cifar100-r100/FedAvg/bal} for FedAvg, while \R{t2/r100/strongest_phrase} \ours on the seeds both have (\R{t2/cifar10-r100/margin_seeds} on CIFAR-10-LT,
\R{t2/cifar100-r100/margin_seeds} on CIFAR-100-LT; \cref{tab:shared} averages every seed of each method).} With
one shared model nothing prevents training the whole network end to end on real, augmented data, whereas the head of
\ours trains on frozen features of images without augmentation, from an extractor trained for half of the rounds. We
did not measure the representations in this protocol, so this explanation is not tested. The generator, which is
what lets clients with different models exchange knowledge, is not needed when the models can simply be averaged.
\claim{Here the readout does not help: relative to the heads of \ours it changes the balanced accuracy by
\R{t2/cifar10-r100/FedECS-LT/gain_er} points on CIFAR-10-LT and \R{t2/cifar100-r100/FedECS-LT/gain_er} on
CIFAR-100-LT.} With one shared head trained by every client, the last layer is not fitted to one client's prior, the
failure the readout addresses; we did not test further why it is lower. \whennum{t2/cifar10-r100/fe200_diff}{\claim{Training the extractor of \ours for all 200 rounds changes
the balanced accuracy of its heads on CIFAR-10-LT by \R{t2/cifar10-r100/fe200_diff} points.}}

\paragraph{Comparison with published numbers.} Our baselines run in the authors' code on our splits and report the
model after the last round. Against FedYoYo's evaluation in the same protocol~\cite{yan2025fedyoyo}, which trains
300 rounds, most methods are within about three points of the published values (e.g.\ FedAvg 58.2, CCVR 68.1,
FedETF 68.3, FedYoYo 81.5 on CIFAR-10-LT). FedLTLib~\cite{lin2026fedltlib} reports the best round and gives higher
values than ours for every method. Three methods are clearly below the published values on CIFAR-10-LT in our runs: CReFF
(70.5 in~\cite{yan2025fedyoyo}), Fed-GraB (70.6) and FedLC (69.0). They run in the benchmark's code with its common
settings (learning rate 0.1, batch size 32, ten local epochs), which differ from those in the methods' own papers; we
did not tune them and report them as obtained. FedYoYo's evaluation trains 300 rounds and ours 200.
