\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Main comparison: different client models}\label{sec:res-main}

\Cref{tab:main} gives the balanced accuracy of every method on the long-tailed benchmarks (\R{main/datasets}): \ours
with its readout, its heads before the readout (heads only), and every other method without the readout; \cref{tab:paired}
gives the paired difference of \ours to each method. \claim{\ours has the highest balanced accuracy on
\R{main/nbest_of} benchmarks. Its margin over the strongest other method is \R{main/cifar10/margin} points on
CIFAR-10-LT (over \R{main/cifar10/strongest}) and \R{main/cifar100/margin} on CIFAR-100-LT (over
\R{main/cifar100/strongest}), with Holm-corrected $p=\R{main/cifar10/strongest_p}$ and
$\R{main/cifar100/strongest_p}$.} \claim{Its heads alone are \R{main/cifar10/heads_margin} and
\R{main/cifar100/heads_margin} points ahead of the strongest other method (\R{main/cifar10/heads_strongest} and
\R{main/cifar100/heads_strongest}; $p=\R{main/cifar10/heads_strongest_p}$ and $\R{main/cifar100/heads_strongest_p}$).} The readout accounts
for the rest; whether the rest of the method still helps once every method has the readout is examined below. \claim{On CIFAR-100-LT, \R{main/cifar100/FedECS-LT/nzero} classes on average are recognised by none of the
ten client models of \ours (\R{main/cifar100/FedECS-LT (heads only)/nzero} before the readout), against
\R{main/cifar100/strongest_nzero} for \R{main/cifar100/strongest}.} \claim{Against GeFL-F, the method it extends, the
difference is \R{main/cifar10/diff/GeFL-F} points \R{main/cifar10/ci/GeFL-F} on CIFAR-10-LT and
\R{main/cifar100/diff/GeFL-F} \R{main/cifar100/ci/GeFL-F} on CIFAR-100-LT.}

\paragraph{Other model-heterogeneous methods} The model-heterogeneous methods that
exchange prototypes or shared sub-networks (FedProto, FedTGP, FedGH, LG-FedAvg, FedMRL) were designed for label skew
without a global long tail; \claim{under the long tail their balanced accuracy is close to or below that of Local};
none of them gives an architecture information about the classes its clients lack. \claim{FedProto, FedTGP and FedGen are best in the first rounds and lose
\R{main/cifar10/FedProto/gefl_drop}, \R{main/cifar10/FedTGP/gefl_drop} and \R{main/cifar10/FedGen/gefl_drop} points of
accuracy by the last round on CIFAR-10-LT (\R{main/cifar100/FedProto/gefl_drop}, \R{main/cifar100/FedTGP/gefl_drop}
and \R{main/cifar100/FedGen/gefl_drop} on CIFAR-100-LT); they are also the weakest on their clients' own class mixes,
on which HtFLlib scores such personal models (\cref{tab:app-local}). The heads of \ours, too, are past their best
round at the last one, by \R{main/cifar10/FedECS-LT (heads only)/gefl_drop} points on CIFAR-10-LT and
\R{main/cifar100/FedECS-LT (heads only)/gefl_drop} on CIFAR-100-LT; \cref{tab:app-gefl} gives every method's best
round, as GeFL reports it (the readout is computed once, after the last round).}

\paragraph{Accuracy on the clients' own class mixes} \claim{On the clients' own class mixes, \ours is below most
other federated methods, and lower with its readout than with its heads only. There \ours reaches \R{main/cifar10/FedECS-LT/local} on CIFAR-10-LT and \R{main/cifar100/FedECS-LT/local} on CIFAR-100-LT
(heads only: \R{main/cifar10/FedECS-LT (heads only)/local} and \R{main/cifar100/FedECS-LT (heads only)/local}), below
\R{main/cifar10/local_n_above} of the \R{main/cifar10/local_n_other} other federated methods on CIFAR-10-LT and
\R{main/cifar100/local_n_above} of \R{main/cifar100/local_n_other} on CIFAR-100-LT; \R{main/local_above} are above it
on both (\cref{tab:app-local}).}

\paragraph{The same readout for every method} The readout uses only a head's body, so it can be added to every method
of the GeFL-F pipeline. \Cref{tab:readout} compares these methods with every last layer set by the same readout, so
that the remaining differences are those of the bodies, which the other components of \ours train. \claim{With the
same readout the gap to every method shrinks: \ours remains
\R{main/cifar10/er_diff/GeFL-F} points ahead of GeFL-F on CIFAR-10-LT and \R{main/cifar100/er_diff/GeFL-F} on
CIFAR-100-LT, and \R{main/cifar10/er_margin} and \R{main/cifar100/er_margin} points ahead of the strongest other method
(\R{main/cifar10/er_strongest} and \R{main/cifar100/er_strongest}). The differences to the strongest
other method are not significant after Holm's correction ($p=\R{main/cifar10/er_strongest_p}$ and
$\R{main/cifar100/er_strongest_p}$), although on CIFAR-100-LT the uncorrected 95\% interval
\R{main/cifar100/er_strongest_ci} excludes zero (CIFAR-10-LT: \R{main/cifar10/er_strongest_ci}). On CIFAR-100-LT the
differences to GeFL-F and FedDF remain significant with the readout ($p=\R{main/cifar100/er_p/GeFL-F}$ and
$\R{main/cifar100/er_p/FedDF}$). Most of the margin over the published methods is therefore the readout's, and
three seeds do not show whether the other components of \ours also train a better body than the strongest other
method.}

\subsection{One shared model}\label{sec:res-shared}

\Cref{tab:shared} gives the results in the FedLTLib protocol. \claim{Here several methods are
stronger: \R{t2/cifar10-r100/above_list} are above \ours on CIFAR-10-LT and \R{t2/cifar100-r100/above_list} on
CIFAR-100-LT, and \R{t2/cifar10-r100/n_below} and \R{t2/cifar100-r100/n_below} methods are below it. At imbalance
ratio 100, \ours reaches \R{t2/cifar10-r100/FedECS-LT/bal} on CIFAR-10-LT and
\R{t2/cifar100-r100/FedECS-LT/bal} on CIFAR-100-LT, against \R{t2/cifar10-r100/FedAvg/bal} and
\R{t2/cifar100-r100/FedAvg/bal} for FedAvg, while \R{t2/r100/strongest_phrase} \ours on the seeds both have (seeds \R{t2/cifar10-r100/margin_seeds} on CIFAR-10-LT and
\R{t2/cifar100-r100/margin_seeds} on CIFAR-100-LT; \cref{tab:shared} averages every seed of each method).} A possible
explanation is that with one shared model the baselines train the whole network end to end on real, augmented data,
whereas \ours trains its head on frozen features (\cref{sec:setup-protocols}). We did not measure the representations
in this protocol, so this explanation is not tested.
\claim{Here the readout does not help: relative to the heads of \ours it changes the balanced accuracy by
\R{t2/cifar10-r100/FedECS-LT/gain_er} points on CIFAR-10-LT and \R{t2/cifar100-r100/FedECS-LT/gain_er} on
CIFAR-100-LT.} With one shared head trained by every client, the last layer is not fitted to one client's label prior, the
failure the readout addresses; we did not test further why it is lower. \whennum{t2/cifar10-r100/fe200_diff}{\claim{Training the extractor of \ours for all 200 rounds changes
the balanced accuracy of its heads on CIFAR-10-LT by \R{t2/cifar10-r100/fe200_diff} points.}}

\paragraph{Comparison with published numbers} Our baselines run in the authors' code on our splits and report the
model after the last round. Against FedYoYo's evaluation in the same protocol~\cite{yan2025fedyoyo}, most methods are within about three points of the published values (e.g.\ FedAvg 58.2, CCVR 68.1,
FedETF 68.3, FedYoYo 81.5 on CIFAR-10-LT). FedLTLib~\cite{lin2026fedltlib} reports the best round and gives higher
values than ours for every method. Three methods are further below the published values on CIFAR-10-LT in our runs: CReFF
(70.5 in~\cite{yan2025fedyoyo}), Fed-GraB (70.6) and FedLC (69.0). They run in the benchmark's code with its common
settings (learning rate 0.1, batch size 32, ten local epochs), which differ from those in the methods' own papers; we
did not tune them and report them as obtained. FedYoYo's evaluation trains 300 rounds and ours 200.
