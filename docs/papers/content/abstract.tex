% Abstract (Elsevier: at most 250 words; IEEE: 150-250 words, one paragraph, no citations). Numbers are slots.
With different client architectures and long-tailed, skewed labels, federated learning loses most of its
rare-class accuracy. We study generator-based model-heterogeneous federated learning, where
clients share a frozen extractor and a federated conditional feature generator, and each architecture trains a
head. We locate three candidate causes: Adam with coupled weight decay shrinks the generator's
class-specific parameters where a class is absent; each head's last layer fits one client's label prior; and generated
features that do not carry their label penalise the spread of the logits. We propose FedECS-LT, which replaces failing
estimates by class statistics that secure aggregation sums exactly: a generator anchored to the class means, moment
correction of generated features, and, as its last step, a class-balanced linear readout of every architecture's last
layer. Its heads add logit adjustment, consensus soft labels and ten synthetic epochs per round. With ten architectures, one client each,
FedECS-LT reaches \R{main/cifar10/FedECS-LT/bal}\,\% balanced accuracy on CIFAR-10-LT and
\R{main/cifar100/FedECS-LT/bal}\,\% on CIFAR-100-LT, \R{main/cifar10/margin} and \R{main/cifar100/margin} points above
the strongest of \R{main/nbaselines_w} other methods without the readout (Holm-corrected
$p=\R{main/cifar10/strongest_p}$ and $\R{main/cifar100/strongest_p}$). Most of this margin is the readout's: with the
same readout added to every method of the GeFL-F pipeline, FedECS-LT leads GeFL-F by \R{main/cifar10/er_diff/GeFL-F}
and \R{main/cifar100/er_diff/GeFL-F} points ($p=\R{main/cifar10/er_p/GeFL-F}$ and $\R{main/cifar100/er_p/GeFL-F}$) and
the strongest method by \R{main/cifar10/er_margin} and \R{main/cifar100/er_margin}, unresolved with three seeds, at
\R{cost/cifar10/comm_ratio_bsm} times the communication of locally logit-adjusted heads. On
\R{nat/nsettings_w} naturally imbalanced datasets the same-readout margins are \R{nat/er_margin_lo} to
\R{nat/er_margin_hi} points. With one shared model, \R{t2/cifar10-r100/n_above} other methods are better on CIFAR-10-LT.
