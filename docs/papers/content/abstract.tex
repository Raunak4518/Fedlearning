Federated learning across clients that run different architectures and hold long-tailed, skewed label distributions
loses most of its accuracy on rare classes. We study generator-based model-heterogeneous federated learning, where
clients share a frozen extractor and a federated conditional feature generator, and each architecture trains its own
head. The loss has three sources: Adam with coupled weight decay shrinks the generator's class-specific
parameters where a class is absent; each head's last layer fits one client's label prior; and generated
features that do not carry their label penalise the spread of the logits. We propose FedECS-LT, which replaces failing
estimates by class statistics that secure aggregation sums exactly: a generator anchored to the class means, moment
correction of generated features, and, as its last step, a class-balanced linear readout that sets every
architecture's last layer from aggregated embedding sums. Its heads train with logit adjustment and consensus soft
labels. With ten architectures, FedECS-LT reaches a balanced accuracy of \R{main/cifar10/FedECS-LT/bal}\,\% on
CIFAR-10-LT and \R{main/cifar100/FedECS-LT/bal}\,\% on CIFAR-100-LT, \R{main/cifar10/margin} and \R{main/cifar100/margin}
points above the strongest of \R{main/nbaselines_w} other methods as published (Holm-corrected
$p=\R{main/cifar10/strongest_p}$ and $\R{main/cifar100/strongest_p}$). Most of this margin is the readout's: with the
same readout added to every method of the GeFL-F pipeline, FedECS-LT leads GeFL-F by \R{main/cifar10/er_diff/GeFL-F}
and \R{main/cifar100/er_diff/GeFL-F} points ($p=\R{main/cifar10/er_p/GeFL-F}$ and $\R{main/cifar100/er_p/GeFL-F}$) and
the strongest method by \R{main/cifar10/er_margin} and \R{main/cifar100/er_margin}, unresolved with three seeds.
It is also best on \R{nat/nbest_of} naturally imbalanced datasets, by at most \R{nat/er_margin_hi} points over the
same-readout methods. With one shared model, the strongest long-tail methods are better.
