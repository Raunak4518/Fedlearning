Abstract
Federated learning across clients that run dierent model architectures and
hold long-tailed, skewed label distributions loses most of its accuracy on rare
classes. We study generator-based model-heterogeneous federated learning,
where clients share a frozen extractor and a federated conditional feature
generator, and each architecture trains its own head. We trace the loss to
three places: Adam with coupled weight decay shrinks the generator's class-
specic parameters on clients that lack a class; each head's last layer is tted
to one client's label prior; and synthetic features that do not carry their la-
bel penalise the spread of the logits. We propose FedECS-LT, which replaces
each of these estimates by an exact class statistic computed under secure ag-
gregation: a mean-anchored generator, class-count weighted averaging when
classes are poorly separated, moment correction of generated features, logit-
adjusted heads trained on consensus soft labels, and a class-balanced readout
computed from aggregated sums. With ten client architectures, FedECS-LT
reaches 42.8 ± 2.0 balanced accuracy on CIFAR-10-LT and 14.3 ± 0.6 on
CIFAR-100-LT, +5.2 and +4.3 points above the strongest of eleven other
methods (paired tests: signicant on CIFAR-100-LT, not on CIFAR-10-LT);
when every method is given the same exact readout, the margins shrink to
+1.4 and +1.5 points and are not signicant. It also has the highest balanced
accuracy on all six datasets with natural class imbalance. With one model
shared by all clients, end-to-end long-tail methods are stronger. All methods
share the same client splits.

1. Introduction
    Federated learning (FL) trains a model on data that stay with their own-
ers [1, 2]. Two properties of real deployments make it hard. The participants
run dierent networks on dierent hardware, so their parameters cannot be
averaged. Their label distributions dier and follow a long tail, so the rarest
classes are held by few participants and in small numbers. Each property has
its own line of work: model-heterogeneous FL [3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8] lets clients with
dierent architectures share knowledge through prototypes, logits, generators
or shared sub-networks, and federated long-tailed learning [9, 10, 11, 12, 13]
corrects the bias that a skewed, long-tailed label distribution induces in a
single shared model. Little is known about the two together, although most
deployments that have one also have the other.
    We study generator-based model-heterogeneous FL, in the form of GeFL-
F [8]. Its clients rst train a small feature extractor by federated averaging
(FedAvg) and freeze it; they then train a conditional feature generator by
FedAvg; nally every client trains a head of its own architecture on generated
features and on its own real features. The generator is the only channel
through which an architecture learns about classes its clients lack. GeFL-F
was evaluated on balanced clients. Under long-tailed label skew we nd that
it loses most of its accuracy on the rare classes, and we ask where.

Where the rare classes are lost.. Because a client partition is a pure label
shift, federation cannot bias class-conditional quantities by itself (Section 3);
it can lose rare classes only through the optimiser, through parameters tied
to a client's label prior, and through sample size. We locate three such losses
(Section 4).
  1. The generator. With Adam [14] and coupled weight decay, a client that
     lacks a class moves the generator's class-specic conditioning parame-
     ters toward zero at Adam's full step size, while the clients that hold the
     class barely grow them. Flat averaging then shrinks the rare classes'
     parameters round after round.
  2. The last layer. Each head is trained on one client's real data, so its
     last layer ts that client's label prior. Per-client logit corrections (logit
     adjustment [15], FedLC [16], FedRS [17], Balanced Softmax [18]) are


                                       2
     exact reparametrisations of such a head: training with them is plain
     training from a shifted initial bias, so they bring in no information
     about the classes the client lacks.
  3. The synthetic data. When generated features do not carry their label,
     the synthetic phase of head training penalises the spread of the logits
     instead of teaching the missing classes.

Our method.. FedECS-LT (Exact Class Statistics for Long-Tailed FL) re-
places each estimate that fails by a class statistic that secure aggregation [19,
20] computes exactly: the per-class counts, sums and squared norms of the
features over all clients. The generator is anchored to the exact class means
and has no class-specic parameter for the decay to shrink; when the classes
are too poorly separated for an anchor, a gate computed from the same
statistics falls back to class-specic parameters averaged over their holders
only (class-count weighted averaging). Generated features are rescaled to
the exact class means and spreads. Heads are trained with logit adjustment
and with consensus soft labels, the averaged prediction of all architectures
on each generated feature. After training, every architecture's last layer is
replaced by a class-balanced linear readout computed from aggregated sums
of its embeddings. None of these statistics is new on its own: aggregated
class statistics underlie classier calibration [21], closed-form federated clas-
siers [22, 23] and prototype methods [3], and statistics-conditioned gener-
ators have been proposed for heterogeneous clients [24]. What we add is
the diagnosis of which parameters fail under a long tail and the use of exact
statistics at exactly those places, including a readout per architecture that
needs statistics pooled across architectures.

Evaluation.. We re-implemented the pipeline and every comparison in the
Flower framework [25], with the published methods taken from their authors'
or library code (HtFLlib [26], LongTailLib/FedLTLib [27]). We evaluate in
two protocols: the model-heterogeneous setting of GeFL-F (ten client archi-
tectures; long-tailed CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 and, with natural class imbal-
ance, DermaMNIST, OrganAMNIST, BloodMNIST, PathMNIST, EMNIST
(ByClass) and FER-2013; eleven other methods) and the standard federated
long-tailed protocol with one shared ResNet-8 (CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100-
LT at imbalance ratio 100; eleven other methods). Every result is the mean
over three seeds with paired tests and a Holm correction, and every metric
is logged per class.

2. Related work
Model-heterogeneous federated learning.. When clients run dierent architec-
tures, their parameters cannot be averaged and knowledge is exchanged in
another form. Distillation methods exchange predictions on public or syn-
thetic inputs (FedMD [28], FedDF [29]). Prototype methods exchange class
means of embeddings (FedProto [3]) or learn global prototypes on the server
(FedTGP [4]). Other methods share a part of the network: the head (LG-
FedAvg [30], FedGH [6]) or a small homogeneous model next to each client's
own (FedMRL [7]). Generator-based methods train a generator against the
ensemble of client heads on the server (FedGen [5], FedFTG [31]), train gen-
erators from feature-distribution statistics that the clients send to the server
(FedVTC [24], which targets generalisation to unseen data rather than long-
tailed classes), or train a conditional generator on the clients by FedAvg
(GeFL, GeFL-F [8]). HtFLlib [26] benchmarks these methods under la-
bel skew but not under a long-tailed global distribution. FedECS-LT keeps
GeFL-F's three-stage design and changes how its class-specic quantities are
estimated.

Federated learning under label skew and long tails.. With one shared model,
the classier is the most biased part under label skew. Classier calibra-
tion re-trains it on virtual features drawn from aggregated class statistics
(CCVR [21]) or on federated features learned on the server (CReFF [9]);
closed-form classiers are computed from aggregated sums (Fed3R [22], a
ridge-regression classier, and FedNCM [23], nearest class means); our read-
out is a class-balanced linear discriminant of the same kind, computed for
each architecture's embeddings; FedETF xes the classier to a simplex
equiangular tight frame [32]. Loss corrections use each client's label fre-
quencies (FedLC [16], FedRS [17], FedRoD [33]), and long-tail methods
re-balance gradients (Fed-GraB [10]), decouple a generic and a local head
(FedLoGe [11]), combine prototype alignment with classier rectication
(RUCR [13]) or distil between augmented views of local data (FedYoYo [12]).
FedLTLib [27] benchmarks them in one protocol, which we follow for the one-
shared-model setting. Class-specic weights are also known to degrade when
clients lack classes (FedAwS [34], FedRS). These methods assume one archi-
tecture; their readout ideas apply to each of our architectures only if the
statistics are pooled across architectures, which FedECS-LT does.

Long-tailed learning, optimisers and synthetic data.. FedECS-LT builds on
logit adjustment [15], eective numbers of samples [35] and the decoupling
of representation and classier [36]. The generator analysis rests on the
dierence between coupled L2 regularisation and decoupled weight decay in
Adam [37, 14]. Class-conditional generators can collapse on classes with little
data even centrally [38], and synthetic training data help only as far as they
carry their labels [39]. Generators matched to kernel mean embeddings of
the data have been used for private data release [40].

The pipeline of GeFL-F.. GeFL-F [8] trains in three stages.
   1. Stage I. A small feature extractor ϕ is trained together with the heads
      by FedAvg and then frozen. Every client maps its images to features
      h = ϕ(x).
   2. Stage II. A conditional variational autoencoder [44] G(h | y) is trained
      on the features by FedAvg, with Adam and coupled weight decay. The
      label enters through a linear projection whose c-th column, the condi-
      tioning row rc , is the only class-specic parameter.
   3. Stage III. In every round each client trains its head for Ts epochs on
      generated features with uniformly drawn labels and then for Tr epochs
      on its real features; heads of the same architecture are averaged.

A client split is a pure label shift.. Within a class, the split assigns samples to
clients uniformly at random. So for every client k and class c with nkc > 0, the
client's class-c samples are a uniformly random subset of all class-c samples,
and Pk (x | y) = P (x | y); the same holds for the features h = ϕ(x), since
ϕ is shared. Every client's data therefore point at the centralised target of
any class-conditional quantity: a class mean, a class's conditioning row, a
readout. Federation can make such a quantity noisier or slower to learn, but
it can make it biased only through (i) the optimiser, (ii) parameters coupled


                                                 6
to a client's label prior, such as biases and last layers, and (iii) what the
synthetic data teach. Section 4 locates each.

Secure aggregation.. Secure aggregation [19, 20] lets the server learn the sum
of the clients' vectors and nothing else about any single vector. A statistic
that is a sum over clients of per-class quantities is therefore computed exactly,
and equals its value on the pooled data, whatever the partition. FedECS-LT
uses only such statistics.

4. Where generator-based heterogeneous FL loses rare classes
   This section locates the three losses that FedECS-LT repairs. Each comes
with a short exact statement (proofs in Appendix A) and a measurement on
the GeFL-F runs of Section 7.

4.2. The last layer: logit corrections are reparametrisations
Proposition 3 (logit corrections are reparametrisations). Let a head have a
free last-layer bias b, trained by SGD without decay on b, and let ak ∈ RC be

a larger learning rate on those rows; the body follows the same trajectory.

optimality guarantee. It needs one extra round and C(de + 1) + d2e numbers
per architecture and client, and every client must run a forward pass (no
training) of every architecture's body on its own features once; a client that
cannot do so would be left out of the sums. We report every method both
as trained and with this readout (exact readout, ER).

FedECS-LT and the strongest baseline is therefore smaller with the readout
than without it; we report both, and the comparison with the readout is the
one that measures the representation.

9. Conclusion
    Generator-based model-heterogeneous federated learning loses rare
classes in three places: the optimiser's treatment of clients that lack a class,
a last layer tted to one client's prior, and synthetic features that do not
carry their label. Each can be described precisely, and each can be repaired
with class statistics that secure aggregation computes up to its quantisa-
tion. FedECS-LT, which applies these repairs together, improves balanced
accuracy and in particular the recall of the rarest classes when clients have
dierent models; with one model shared by all clients, end-to-end long-tail
methods remain stronger. Its code, logs and every gure's data are public.

Appendix B. Experimental details
Models.. The extractor, the ten head architectures (CNN-1 to CNN-10) and
GeFL-F's conditional VAE follow GeFL-F [8]; heads are trained by SGD with
learning rate 0.1. Rounds of the three stages: 20/100/50 on MNIST and
Fashion-MNIST, 50/100/100 on SVHN and 50/200/100 on CIFAR-10 and
CIFAR-100; with natural imbalance, 20/100/50 on OrganAMNIST and EM-
NIST and 50/100/100 on DermaMNIST, BloodMNIST, PathMNIST, GT-
SRB and FER-2013.

Reproduction check.. Before the comparison, the Flower implementation was
checked against the original implementation on MNIST-LT and CIFAR-10-
LT with seed 3: the client splits were identical (every client's class counts),
every client training step matched weight for weight in unit tests, and the
balanced accuracy of GeFL-F and FedECS-LT was within seed noise of the
original runs (supplementary material).

GeFL's own setting.. In GeFL's setting (balanced training data divided
evenly among 10 clients, 3 seeds, our earlier implementation), GeFL-F
reaches 56.8% on CIFAR-10 after the last round and 59.3% with GeFL's
measure, against 55.9% reported by GeFL [8]; FedECS-LT reaches 57.8%
and 60.0%. On CIFAR-100, which GeFL does not report, GeFL-F reaches
12.5% and FedECS-LT 18.0%.

Evaluation.. Models are evaluated every ve rounds on the test set before
and after aggregation; the nal model is the one after the last round (no
selection on the test or the validation set). Methods with personal heads (LG-
FedAvg, FedGH, FedGen and the other HtFLlib methods) are scored with
each client's head after its last local training; HtFLlib scores LG-FedAvg,
FedGH and FedGen right after the server's update instead. FedProto and
FedTGP classify by the distance to class prototypes; their probabilities for
the calibration metrics are a softmax of the negative distances.
